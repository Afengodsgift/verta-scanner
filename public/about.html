How Verta scores a store

Verta reads your product pages the way Google does, then reports what is missing, why it matters and how to fix it.

What the check does

Verta opens your homepage, robots.txt and sitemap, finds product pages, reads their structured data and visible text, looks for shipping and returns pages, and checks for a cart and checkout. It reads only public pages, with a clearly labelled visitor (VertaScanner). It never logs in and never places an order.

These weights are our own judgement, not an industry standard. Structured data counts most because it is what Google Shopping and rich results read. They will change as Verta learns from real stores.

Reading the score

80 to 100Complete. Product data is in good shape. Any critical problem caps the label at Mostly complete.
60 to 79Mostly complete. Most of the data is in place, with meaningful gaps.
40 to 59Needs fixes. Significant gaps can keep products out of Google Shopping and AI search.
0 to 39Hidden. Search engines and AI assistants can barely read the store.
Incomplete scanNo product page could be read, so only the storefront was scored.

"Couldn't tell" and "Not tested"

When the evidence is thin, for example a page built by scripts that the scanner cannot run, Verta says it couldn't tell instead of failing the check. Those checks are left out of the score. An area shown as Not tested was not scored, which is different from scoring zero. Optional extras such as an llms.txt file are shown for information and are not scored.

What Verta does not test

  • Checkout, payment and anything behind a login.
  • Pages built only by scripts the scanner cannot run.
  • Your actual ranking, traffic or sales. The score measures how complete and readable your product data is. It does not predict results.

The score is our own checklist, not a Google rating or a certification.
